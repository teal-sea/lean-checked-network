# The smallest networks the kernel accepts, and a six-phase cycle

The construction is the one `README.md` in this directory proves in general: Gromov's analytic
weights with the phases fixed so that the unwanted waves cancel exactly. This file records what the
other session of 2026-10-16 added to it: a smaller phase cycle, the smallest networks found at 113 and
257, a composite modulus, and a planted fault. The theorem (`mathlib/`) and its three instances tied
to it (`instances/`: p = 7 with all frequencies, p = 113 with Nanda's five, p = 113 with all 56) are
not repeated here. Status per line: **kernel-checked** (Lean 4.35.0-rc2's kernel, standard axioms,
replayed by `leanchecker`), **measured** (numpy), **derived** (on paper, checked numerically).

## Six phases per frequency instead of eight

The grid in `instances/` uses four phases for the first input and two for the second, eight neurons
per frequency. A single cycle does it with six: `φ₁ = 2πj/6`, `φ₂ = 4φ₁`, `j = 0 … 5`. Each of the eight
unwanted wave families carries a phase `uφ₁ + vφ₂` with `(u, v)` in `{(1,−1), (3,1), (−1,1), (1,3), (2,2),
(0,−2), (2,0), (1,1)}`, so `(u + 4v)·2πj/6` with `u + 4v` in `{−3, 7, 3, 13, 10, −8, 2, 5}`, never a multiple
of 6; every family sums to zero over the cycle and the score is exactly `3·Σ_{k∈S} cos(2πk(a+b−c)/p)`.
Checked numerically to floating-point noise (largest deviation `4.5 × 10⁻¹³`) before any integer
network was built (**derived**). Five phases, four phases or three cannot work with `φ₂ = rφ₁` for any
`r`: one of the eight residues always vanishes.

`{39, 45}` is the first survivor, in order of gap, of all 1,540 two-frequency sets at 8 bits; no single
frequency survives at 8 bits (**measured**, `search_min.py`). `{114, 125}` is the same search over the
8,128 pairs at 257. One frequency does add mod 113 with 10-bit weights (six neurons, `M = 1023`;
**measured** only, outside the fast evaluator's weight range). The gap `g(S) = |S| − max_{s≢0} Σ_{k∈S} cos(2πks/p)`
is the quantity that decides how small a network can be: an exponential sum over the frequency set.

Generated by `make_fixed_phase6.py` (six phases) and `make_fixed_phase.py` (the grid) into `network/*.npz`,
then `gen_lean.py`; checked by `run_lean_fixed_phase.sh`, whose output is `lean_run_fixed_phase.log` at
the repository root. That log is the run of 2026-10-16 and also covers three networks since removed
from `lean/` as duplicates of `instances/` (p = 7 all frequencies, p = 113 Nanda's five at `M = 127`,
p = 113 all frequencies at `M = 25`, the last built in 4,011 s with its row files one at a time) and the
count theorems since restated through `sweep/` (see `sweep/tests/learning-curve-2026-10-16.md`).

## The planted fault

`FaultFixed12` is `FixedPhase12` with one read-out weight raised by 22 (`W2[104][7]`, from 1 to 23, still
inside [-128, 127]); that flips its tightest pair (11, 35) to the answer 104 and 15 others, 16 of 12,769
wrong (**measured**). `lean/expected_fail/FaultFixed12Row.lean` is the row-11 proof for that network;
`run_lean_fixed_phase.sh` requires it to fail with a kernel error, and it does: *(kernel) application
type mismatch*. The 12- and 16-neuron networks' margins are below the most one weight change can shift a
score, which is why the fault is planted in the smallest one; `instances/` plants its own in the three
larger ones.

## What the kernel costs

Build of the sixteen row files plus the table check on 4 cores: 12 neurons 139 s, 16 neurons 150 s,
40 neurons 217 s (measured before that network was removed), the trained 128-neuron network about 13
minutes per the root README. A floor of about two minutes plus a few seconds per neuron, as the packed
evaluator predicts; a 448-wide weight table elaborates at about 5 GB, so wide networks build one file
at a time (`instances/README.md` measured the same).
