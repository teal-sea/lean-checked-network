# Prior-art search, 2026-11-01 (workflow wf_c0fc36e6-035: three searchers, three adversarial refuters)

## one-step

C2 VERDICT: PARTLY KNOWN. The mechanism is known: the readout gradient carries the sum of the two input phases, so each neuron's gap phi_a + phi_b - phi_out is driven to 0, and the random initial spectrum picks the winning frequency. The target family is also known (Gromov's random-phase solution). Not found in anything I searched: a single discrete Adam/sign step from N(0,1)/sqrt(H) init that completes the alignment, so that the rounded one-wave projection is exact on all p^2 pairs while the net is at chance. Also not found: the init-only and update-only controls.
Scope: about 20 web searches and arXiv-API phrase queries. I opened about 25 papers and searched the full text of the closest ones.

Closest on mechanism (all use continuous-time gradient flow from small init, with alignment taking many steps):
- Wang, Yao, Liu, Lu, Xu 2610.11833 (Oct 2026). Same untied architecture, W sigma(U e_a + V e_b). The training statistics are cyclic shifts that the DFT diagonalizes. In the per-mode flow the readout mode grows with U_r V_r, and delta = alpha + beta - gamma goes to 0 (App. E.4). Near init the readout flow is Hebbian (App. D.3). They use Adam with warmup, P=113. This is the closest paper, but it makes no step-1 claim and has no projection test.
- He, Wang, Chen, Yang 2602.16849 (known). Their input weights are tied, so the condition is psi = 2 phi. Misalignment goes to 0 as t goes to infinity. The winning frequency is the one with the smallest initial misalignment, and it dominates in O~(log p/(p kappa_init)) (Cor. 6.1). The lottery contours are taken after 10,000 steps (Fig. 4), and experiments use AdamW at lr 1e-4. It contains C2's mechanism but not the one-step result.
- He, Wang, Zhang, Chen, Yang 2606.02993: the same result for finite groups. Phase alignment happens at exponential rates under projected gradient flow.
- Kunin et al. 2506.06489 (AGF): Gromov's (u,v,w) quadratic net with vanishing init. Thm 5.1: single-frequency cosines with s_u + s_v = s_w + s_x maximize the initial utility (s_x = 0 for one-hot). Alignment happens across loss plateaus.
- Tian 2509.21519 (2025, not the known 2410.01779): the early backprop gradient is t times a label-kernel term acting on the random features. For modular addition the energy maxima are single-frequency. Gradient flow only.
- Ding et al. 2405.17420: the network at init holds many Fourier circles, and the ones with high initial signal and gradient survive.
- Lin 2510.00468: quadratic no-bias MLP trained with MSE. The eNTK's alignment with the a+b and a-b modes is small at init and rises through grokking. This fits your init-only negative.
- Gromov 2301.02679 (known): your projection lands in his family (random phases, phi1 + phi2 = phi3, width adds redundancy). On the first overfit he writes "The periodic structure in weights and activations does not form at that point." That is a direct contrast worth citing.

One step / Adam (none of these are on modular arithmetic):
- Ba et al. 2205.01445 and Dandi et al. 2305.18270: one (giant) GD step on the first layer, for Gaussian single- and multi-index targets, aligns neurons with the target directions.
- Damian-Lee-Soltanolkotabi 2206.15144, Alg. 1, sets lambda1 = 1/eta1, so W(1) = -eta1 grad L. The init is erased and the readout is trained afterwards. That is the opposite of C2, where the update alone fails.
- Yang & Littwin 2308.01814 note that Adam's first step is essentially sign(gradient) and give its infinite-width theory.
- Liu et al. 2501.12391 (Fig. 13): SignGD on modular addition (p=59) nearly removes grokking. Neither paper analyzes step 1 on this task.

No first-step content:
- Mohamadi et al. 2407.12332: in the kernel regime a model needs a constant fraction of the data.
- Rubin-Seroussi-Ringel 2310.03789: Gromov setting, equilibrium theory with Langevin training, no dynamics.
- Mallinar et al. 2407.20199: random circulant features let kernels generalize.
- Gu/Li et al. 2402.09469: max-margin analysis only.
- I did not re-open the other papers on the known list.

C1 (my angle touches it):
- Kumar & Sankaranarayanan 2610.22593: a readout refit on the training data generalizes before the network does. This uses constructed init, special updates and wd > 0.
- He et al. describe the network at the end of memorization as a noisy Fourier solution.
- Swaroop 2603.23784 (known): an idealized model built from a ReLU net trained on noisy data (0.23% accuracy) reaches 95.5%, not exact.

C3 (outside my angle, flag for whoever owns it):
- Wang et al. Fig. 1: GELU, 10% label noise, wd 0 gives 100% train and 0 test accuracy. No mechanism is given.
- He et al. Fig. 6: test accuracy falls from 5% to 0% early in training (p=23, wd 2), then recovers to about 70%.

Inference (mine, not from papers):
- Adam's first step moves each weight by about lr whatever H is, while init std is 1/sqrt(H). So the relative step is lr*sqrt(H): 0.23 at H 512 and 0.32 at H 1024. "One step at 1024 and up, two at 512" may be about step size, not width. Control: set lr proportional to 1/sqrt(H).
- The DFT projection supplies Z_p's cyclic order, so Mohamadi's equivariance bound does not apply. The Hebbian readout step writes phi1 + phi2 into W2. Control: apply the step to W2 only, with E1/E2 frozen at init.

## width-and-wd0

C1 (my angle). Verdict: PARTLY KNOWN.

Known part: keeping only each neuron's dominant frequency, in a net that fails on held-out pairs, recovers a much better adder.
- Liu & Lu 2026, arXiv 2605.18022. This is the closest paper I found and it is not on your list. Two-layer one-hot nets, P=113, ReLU by default (x^2 also run), AdamW wd 0.1, label noise up to 80%. Their "frequency filtration" takes, for each neuron, the largest frequency in its output weights and keeps that same component in its input weights. They report near-perfect test accuracy at 80% noise, better results from bigger models, and every frequency covered once width is at least 513.
  - Gaps vs C1: the memorizers come from label noise at wd>0, and I found no wd-0 run in the text. "Near-perfect" is not exact, and the values are only in figures I could not read. One seed (42), no checkpoint sweep, no integer rounding.
- Swaroop 2026, 2603.23784 (on your known list, and it does contain this part). ReLU, H=256, AdamW wd 1.0, 30% train, p=97. Their per-neuron DFT "idealized model" gets 65-88% held-out at the checkpoint where training accuracy saturates, while the net itself is at about 0.1%. It gets 95.5% from a net trained with 30% noisy labels that scores 0.23%. Never exact, one width, no wd 0.
- Why width matters is known: Gromov 2301.02679 (known), Sec 3.1/4.2. Single-frequency neurons with random phases that obey the phase-sum rule get more accurate as width grows, and reach 100% at some width.

New part: clean labels, wd exactly 0, exact on all p^2 pairs, every seed, every checkpoint from step 100, while the net is below chance. No paper I opened reports exactness, an all-seed result, or checkpoint-by-checkpoint results for a filtered memorizer.

What C1 contradicts (the papers a reviewer would cite against it):
- Gromov, Sec 4.1, same architecture, on the phase where the net first overfits: "The periodic structure in weights and activations does not form at that point."
- Nanda et al. 2301.05217 (known), App. D.1: at λ=0 the excluded loss of a 1-layer transformer never rises, which they read as the generalizing circuit never forming. Their metric uses global key frequencies, not a per-neuron filter.
- Doshi et al. 2310.13061 (known): nets in their memorization phase score at random-guess level on test. At wd 0, wider nets (N=5000) memorize more (Fig. 29). They measure periodicity with the inverse participation ratio (IPR) of the raw weights and never project.

Framing caution: wd 0 alone does not block grokking. Gromov groks with no regularization given enough data. Kumar et al. 2310.06110, App. 8.3, grok at wd 0 (N=100, 90% train, plain GD). State C1 for the 30%-train regime, where the net stays a memorizer through 20k steps.

Lazy/kernel papers (consistent with C1, none project the weights):
- Mohamadi et al. 2407.12332: same x^2 architecture. Overfitting happens with negligible change in the empirical NTK, and nets in the kernel regime provably need a constant fraction of all pairs to generalize. So at large H the memorizer is the init plus a small update, which is the link to C2.
- Kumar et al. 2310.06110 (known): larger width brings nets closer to linearized dynamics; grokking is the move from lazy to rich training.
- Pracher et al. 2609.26679 (your "Pracher 2026"): ReLU, squared loss, and the theory requires wd>0. The NTK gains Fourier structure after training accuracy saturates. No wd-0 runs in the part I read, no weight projection.
- Kumar & Sankaranarayanan 2610.22593: a ridge readout refit on the hidden features reaches 90% held-out before the net does. wd>0, custom optimizer, not exact.
- Manir & Rupa 2603.25009 (your "Manir & Rupa 2026"): at wd 1e-5 every seed memorizes and never groks within budget. Fourier analysis only on nets that grokked.
- Prieto et al. 2501.04697: at wd 0 the nets stall through Softmax Collapse. Fourier analysis only on nets that grok with StableMax.
- He et al. 2602.16849 (known): describes memorization as a perturbed Fourier solution (ReLU, p=23, wd 2.0). No projection test in what I read, which stopped at Sec 5.4.
- Lyu et al. 2311.18817 (abstract only): small nonzero wd, a kernel predictor first and then max-margin.
- Fan et al. 2405.19454: MNIST, wd>0. Not relevant.

C2 (outside my angle). Verdict: NEW that one Adam step gives an exact projection; PARTLY KNOWN that the initial spectrum picks the frequencies.
- He et al. 2602.16849 (known): each neuron's winning frequency is set by its initial spectrum and phase alignment, a lottery-ticket mechanism.
- Wang et al. 2610.11833: derive phase alignment in small-init gradient flow.
- Kunin et al. 2506.06489 (abstract only): small-init nets learn Fourier features one at a time.
- None tests one step or whether a projection is exact.

C3 (outside my angle). Verdict: NEW. Below-chance numbers appear in prior work but nobody explains them, and I found no source for the mechanism.
- Swaroop's nets score 0.11% and 0.23% held-out, with no comment.
- Wang et al. 2610.11833, Fig. 1: a net trained at λ=0 on noisy labels scores 0 on test.
- Wang et al. also count same-row and same-column training pairs that share a label, but only to explain early loss speed, not held-out predictions.

## anti-generalization

C3 VERDICT: PARTLY KNOWN. By part:
- Row/column exclusion as a principle: KNOWN. It is one sentence in arXiv 2610.35036, posted 26 Oct 2026.
- Below-chance held-out accuracy of a memorizing ReLU MLP on a 97-symbol Latin task: PARTLY KNOWN. 2610.35036 shows it on random-Latin controls, and Swaroop's addition numbers show it without comment.
- NEW on what I found: (a) a permanent wd-0 memorizer on addition itself scoring exactly 0/6,586; (b) the measured mechanism, with 99.98% of held-out answers being training labels from the same row or column (no paper measures this reuse); (c) the same net's one-wave projection being wrong on only 5-8 of 9,409 pairs.

CLOSEST PAPERS
1. arXiv 2610.35036, Gu, Zhao, Ma, Zhang, Su, Cao, Hu, "Mapping the Solution-Set Structure of Grokking on Latin Squares".
   - Setup is nearly ours: q=97, 2,823/9,409 cells observed (30%), width-512 ReLU MLP with separate row/column embeddings. Differences: it has biases, uses momentum SGD and wd 0.001.
   - Its 24 controls on random-matching Latin squares fit training and end at held-out median 4.56e-4, range 0 to 1.37e-3. That is 0 to ~9 of 6,586, against chance ~68.
   - It states the argument: "always wrong by the Latin property, so below-chance accuracy is possible."
   - Gaps: it only says "possible" and never measures label reuse. The below-chance runs are random Latin squares, not addition. All its addition runs grok (median 1.000), so it has no addition memorizer.
   - Closest by far. It is concurrent work and must be cited.

2. arXiv 2603.23784, Swaroop 2026 (on the known list). It CONTAINS C3's number but does not remark on it.
   - Setup: ReLU MLP, p=97, width 256, 30% train, AdamW, wd 1.0.
   - Table 2, clean labels, "saturation" checkpoint (train first reaches 99%): real validation accuracy "<0.0001". At noise 0.15-0.25 it is 0.0002-0.0005. All are below 1/97, and the paper calls them only "near zero".
   - At the same checkpoint, idealized square/cosine waves rebuilt from dominant frequencies and phases score 0.69-0.81.
   - This is a weaker cousin of C3's contrast: transient (the net later groks), not exact, and no mechanism.

3. arXiv 2407.20199, Mallinar et al. 2024 (grokking with Recursive Feature Machines). Before the transition, kernel machines sit at "near-zero" and nets "at 0%" test accuracy on modular arithmetic. The abstract calls this "random, near zero". Below chance is visible but treated as chance, with no mechanism.

4. arXiv 2310.13061 does NOT contain C3. Its authors are Doshi, Das, He, Gromov, not "Liao" as the known list says.
   - "Inversion" means test accuracy is above train accuracy because the net outputs the rule's label on corrupted training points. That is the opposite effect.
   - The memorization phase is reported as "random guessing accuracy on test data". No exclusion argument.

5. The established name for this is "anti-learning".
   - Kowalczyk & Chapelle, ALT 2005, LNAI 3734 pp. 78-91, doi 10.1007/11564089_8 (no arXiv). Good training performance but "systematically worse than random performance on the test set". They argue it is a property of the dataset, not overfitting.
   - Lisitsa & Vernitski, "On Strong Anti-learning of Parity", OL2A 2023, CCIS pp. 271-279, doi 10.1007/978-3-031-53036-4_19 (no arXiv). I saw the abstract only in search snippets and could not open the paper, so I am unsure of the details. It reports decision trees on parity falling to 0% test accuracy as the training set grows.
   - This is the same trap as C3: the nearest training points carry a wrong label (Hamming neighbours for parity, same row/column for addition). Suggest framing C3 as anti-learning on a Latin square.

6. arXiv 2406.01652, Austin, Pe'er, Korem 2024. Leave-one-out makes the held-out label anti-correlated with the training fold's label mean. It is a mild cousin: C3 is the extreme case, where the true label is fully excluded from its row and column. The paper does not frame it as below chance.

NOT C3
- "Anti-grokking", Prakash & Martin, arXiv 2506.04434 and 2602.02859: test accuracy collapses late, after grokking (MNIST MLP; transformer at p=113). It falls "back to chance", not below. Their mechanism is "correlation traps".
- Kumar et al. 2310.06110: its "worse than random behavior" is lazy-regime polynomial regression, not modular arithmetic.
- Liu & Lu 2605.18022 (label noise; frequency extraction gives near-perfect test accuracy): no below-chance claim.
- Checked, with no below-chance or exclusion statement: Nanda 2301.05217, Gromov 2301.02679, Morwani 2311.07568, Tian 2410.01779, He et al. 2602.16849, Power 2201.02177, Varma 2309.02390, Lyu 2311.18817, Liu 2205.10343 and 2210.01117, Zhong 2306.17844, Minegishi 2310.19470, Mohamadi 2407.12332, Prieto 2501.04697.

C1: touched only by Swaroop and by Gu's ReLU minimum-norm "carrier" theory. Swaroop shows structure is already present at memorization, but the wave rebuild scores 0.69-0.81, not exact, at wd 1.0. Neither paper has an exact one-wave adder at every checkpoint at wd 0. C1's below-chance held-out accuracy for x^2 (0.3-0.5%) is covered by the same Latin exclusion argument.

C2: nothing found from this angle.

## refute C1

C1 (my angle). Verdict: PARTLY KNOWN. Liu & Lu (arXiv 2605.18022) already has the core idea for clean-label memorizers. What is still new is exactness at wd 0, at every checkpoint and for every seed.

Strongest prior art: Liu & Lu, arXiv 2605.18022, May 2026. It appears on conference listing pages; the venue is unconfirmed. It is not on our known list.
- Method ("frequency filtration", Sec. 4, App. D.1): for each neuron, take the DFT of its output weights and keep the largest frequency. Then keep that same component, real part only, in its input weights too. This is close to the one-wave projection, with two differences: the frequency is chosen from W2 alone, and nothing is rounded.
- Earlier searchers listed clean labels as a gap. They are not one. Numbers below are read off figures I rendered from the paper's SVGs, so they are approximate. Fig. 25 (clean labels, ReLU, AdamW) at train fraction 0.15: the raw net is about 0% on test at every width. After filtration it is about 75% at M=513, rising to about 93% at M=8193. At fraction 0.2 and M=1025: raw about 50%, filtered about 97%. Filtered reaches about 100% from M=2049.
- Fig. 24 (x^2, label noise, 50% train): at 50% noise the raw net scores about 5% on test and the filtered net about 97% from M≈1000. At 40% noise or less, filtered is about 100% from M≈257.
- The text says filtration "also improves test accuracy for models trained on noise-free samples", and also improves x^2 nets.
- Fig. 29: once width is at least 513, every frequency is used by some neuron (P=113).
- Gaps against C1:
  - Default AdamW wd is 0.1. I found no wd 0 anywhere in the text, and every filtration figure uses the default.
  - They say "near-perfect", never exact, never all pairs, and do no rounding.
  - One seed (42), and only the end of training (200k epochs); no checkpoint sweep.
  - x^2 appears only with label noise. The clean memorizer comes from very little data (15%), not from wd 0 at 30%.
  - Below chance is never mentioned, and P=113.
- My inference, not stated by them: with lr 1e-3, wd 0.1 and 200k steps, decoupled decay shrinks the initial weights by about e^-20, so their nets have shed the random init. Yours keep it. An exact projection with the init still present is a different claim.

Known-list papers, checked in full text:
- Swaroop, 2603.23784 (contains a weak form of C1):
  - Table 2, clean labels, at the checkpoint where training accuracy first reaches 99%: the per-neuron dominant-frequency extraction scores 69-81% held-out. That is ReLU, H 256, wd 1.0, 30% train, and not exact.
  - Table 1: hand-built nets with random phases obeying the phase-sum rule average 0.9999-1.0000 at N=512 over 40 seeds. So your H ≥ 512 threshold is the expected random-phase width threshold, not a discovery.
  - Flag for C3: at that same checkpoint, the real nets at 15-30% label noise score 0.02-0.11% held-out (below chance). The paper says nothing about it.
- Gromov, 2301.02679: confirmed. Sec. 4.1 says periodic structure has not formed at first overfit, and Sec. 3.1 says extra width adds copies of each frequency with different random phases. No projection.
- Doshi et al., 2310.13061: the setup is nearly yours (x^2, p=97, AdamW lr 0.01, betas 0.9/0.98, N=500). They prune low-IPR neurons, including at wd 0, but only on noisy-label nets that already generalize (50% train). That is removing neurons, not projecting them, and never in the memorizing phase. App. O: at N=5000 and wd 0 the nets memorize more. They also note that at wd 0, low-IPR neurons cancel the leftover terms; that is as close as they get to C1.
- He et al. 2602.16849 and Wang et al. 2610.11833: a text search found no projection test on a memorizer. Wang's λ=0 net (Fig. 1) scores 0% on test and is never projected.

Checked and not close: 2609.15047, 2606.12966, 2607.13749, 2610.35112, 2609.26679 (NTK theory, needs wd > 0), 2610.22593 (refits the readout, wd > 0).

Still new in C1: wd exactly 0, x^2 with clean labels at 30% train, exact after integer rounding on all p^2 pairs, every seed, every checkpoint from step 100, and the net below chance.
- Cite Liu & Lu as the direct precedent and frame C1 around exactness, wd 0, and holding at every checkpoint. The "from step 100" part ties C1 to C2.
- Control I'd add: apply Liu & Lu's exact filter (frequency from W2 only, no rounding) to your nets, so the difference is measured rather than asserted.

## refute C2

Scope: 11 web searches, 32 arXiv-API phrase queries, 16 OpenAlex full-text phrase queries, keyword scan of 1,157 papers citing 18 core papers (Semantic Scholar; Nanda's first 1,000 only), about 26 pages opened. Only additions and corrections below.

C2. Verdict: PARTLY KNOWN. The mechanism is known, and both negative controls follow from it (my derivation). Measured nowhere: a full-lr first Adam step at standard 1/sqrt(H) init whose rounded one-wave projection is exact on all p^2 pairs while the net is at chance.

1. Wang et al. 2610.11833, closest on mechanism AND setup. Correction: not small init. Experiments use N(0, 1/D) (gamma 0.5, D 2000), the same scale as C2, untied U and V, P 113, Adam/AdamW lr 1e-3 with warmup and cosine decay. Thm 1 / Sec 4.1: each mode's update is a product of the other two, (Fu)_r ~ C (F conj v)_r (Fw)_r. Prop 2: arg U_r + arg V_r - arg W_r -> 0. All gradient flow; first plotted point is epoch 10; the one full-batch GD run (App. C.2, P 7, lr 0.2) logs every 25 epochs. Single-frequency nets appear only as an analytic construction (Prop 3), never as accuracy of a projected trained net.
Adversarial point (my derivation from their coupling, not stated in the paper): it predicts both negative controls. At init the three phases are independent, so the sum rule fails. Update-only: dU, dV, dW carry phases gamma-beta, gamma-alpha, alpha+beta, so their gap is 2(gamma-alpha-beta), still random. It does not by itself predict the one-step positive, which is the part no paper has. Present the negatives as predicted and measured nowhere.

2. He et al. 2602.16849 (known). Sec 3.2 says the final network is "entirely determined by a small subset of Fourier components in its initial parameters". The stronger line, that the predictor is fixed before any gradient step, is on the authors' project page (y-agent.github.io), not in the paper. Predictable from init is not an adder at init, so neither contradicts the init-only control. Experiments: PyTorch default init, AdamW at constant lr 1e-4, no warmup; alignment takes about 4,000 to 8,000 steps (project page); nothing measured in the first ~10 steps; App. A.2 ablations report CE loss, not projected accuracy. Sec 6.2's one-step energy quantity is a theory quantity, not a step-1 experiment.

3. Lin 2510.00468: quadratic no-bias MLP, p 29, width 512, MSE, AdamW. At init the eNTK's top eigenvalue cliff already overlaps the a and b Fourier families; the a+b and a-b families are small at init and rise smoothly into grokking. Consistent with the init-only negative; builds no early predictor.

4. Barak et al. 2207.08799 (known): conceptual analog only. The population gradient has a Fourier gap toward the sparse-parity solution, which SGD amplifies gradually. Different task, no one-step claim. Of the known-list papers I opened (He, Barak), neither contains C2.

5. Mallinar et al. 2407.20199: RFM features become circulant gradually over about 10 iterations; neural nets tracked per epoch; no first-step analysis.

6. Liu et al. 2501.12391: SignGD (Adam with beta1 = beta2 = 0) on mod 59, 80% train, wd 0 reaches about 90% test where Adam stays near zero. No step counts. Nearest hint that sign steps matter.

Checked, not prior art: 2605.31497 (its one-gradient-step theorem trains attention routing on top of a fixed Gromov MLP), 2606.12966 (discrete-log precursor for multiplication, checkpoints every 100 steps), 2606.08985 (classifier-first layerwise flow); phrase false positives 2606.05863, 2608.04451, 2610.35112.

Why step 1 is unexplored rather than tested-and-failed: in the three closest, the first Adam step is tiny relative to init (He: constant lr 1e-4; Wang: warmup; Liu & Lu: 10-step warmup to 1e-3). The earlier step-size reading still needs the lr ~ 1/sqrt(H) control.

C1. No new prior art; earlier gaps confirmed. Liu & Lu 2605.18022 filter only after 200,000 epochs, have no untrained-network control, and report near-perfect, not exact. Swaroop 2603.23784 evaluates the idealized model only at train saturation and at the end, with no untrained control; best extracted 0.9988. Kumar & Sankaranarayanan 2610.22593: the refit first reaches 90% at update 6,500; nothing at steps 0 to 1 in the first 40% I read. Verdict unchanged: PARTLY KNOWN.

C3: outside my angle; nothing found.

## refute C3

Scope: about 16 web searches and 8 arXiv-API phrase queries (anti-learning; below/worse than chance with modular or grokking; Latin square with grokking or memorization). I fetched and grepped the full text of 22 PDFs: the known-list papers I could get, plus 2024-2026 candidates. The Semantic Scholar API returned 429 every time, so I could not crawl citations.

C3 (my angle). Verdict: PARTLY KNOWN.

Closest papers, all opened and checked:
1. arXiv 2610.35036, Gu, Zhao, Ma, Zhang, Su, Cao, Hu (posted 26 Oct 2026).
   - Sec 3.4 states the principle outright: "a symbol already seen in the row or column of a cell is always wrong". It cites the Latin property for this and concludes below-chance accuracy is possible.
   - The intro and Fig 1 show random-matching Latin colourings ending below chance. In App D.3 the 24 controls reach median 4.56e-4 held-out (range 0 to 1.37e-3; chance is 0.0103). Fig 3: a random residual R32 drops exact recovery "to chance or below".
   - Setup (App D.1): q=97, 2823/9409 cells observed, row/column embeddings in R^128 (a bottleneck), ReLU width 512, biases, full-batch momentum SGD, wd 1e-3, 80k updates.
   - Gaps: the below-chance runs are random Latin squares, not addition. All 30 addition-orbit runs grok (median 1.000). It never measures which labels the wrong answers are.
   - Closest by far.
2. arXiv 2610.11833, Wang et al. (Oct 2026). It has every ingredient but points each one elsewhere.
   - Thm 3 uses a shared-coordinate kernel kappa(1) > kappa(0) and notes the label is uniform given a or b. This explains the early loss slope only.
   - App F.2 builds bijective noise (a Latin square not isotopic to addition) to remove label collisions.
   - Fig 1 (GELU, P=113, 80/20 split, 10% label noise, lambda=0): 100% train, 0 test. It never analyzes held-out predictions.
3. arXiv 2602.16849 (known list) contains the observation but not the mechanism.
   - Sec 3.3 and Fig 5b: test accuracy falls from 5% to 0% at p=23 (chance 4.3%).
   - Fig 10: the memorizer fails completely on truly unseen pairs (tied embeddings, 75% train, wd 2).
   - The paper explains this as common-to-rare memorization.
4. Other known-list papers:
   - Varma 2309.02390, Fig 4: ungrokking drops to near-zero test accuracy on addition (P=113), no mechanism.
   - Nanda 2301.05217: worse than chance appears only after ablating key frequencies (Table 4). This is not C3.
   - Doshi 2310.13061: the memorization phase sits at random-guess accuracy.
   - Swaroop 2603.23784: reports a 0.23% model without comment.
